# Exterior algebra

> [!Definition] Exterior Algebra
> Let $R$ be commutative, $M$ be an $R$-module. The **exterior algebra** $\Lambda M$ of $M$ is $T(M)/J$, where $J$ is the ideal of $T(M)$ generated by $\{ x \otimes x:x\in M \}$. 

^8a60a8

Observe that $\Lambda M$ is a graded algebra, with $\Lambda^{0}M\cong R$, $\Lambda^{1}M\cong M$. For $n\geq 0$, $\Lambda^{n}M$ is called the $n$th exterior power of $M$. Since $J$ is [[LEC ALG4 14#^6e4e12|homogeneous]], $\Lambda^{m}M=T^{m}M/J^{m}$, where $J^{m}=J\cap T^{m}(M)$. The image of $x_{1}\otimes\dots \otimes x_{n}$ in $\Lambda M$ is denoted by $x_{1}\wedge\dots \wedge x_{n}$. 

> [!Proposition]
> $x\wedge y=-y\wedge x$ for all $x, y\in M$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > $0=(x+y)\wedge(x+y)=x\wedge y+y\wedge x$. 
> 

^96459f

[[#^96459f]] is only "useful" when $1\ne -1$ in $R$, i.e., $R$ is not an $\mathbb{F}_{2}$-algebra. 

> [!Remark]
> If $2\ne 0$ in $R$, then $J$ can also be generated by $\{ x\otimes y+y\otimes x:x, y\in M \}$. 
> 
> Note that $x\otimes y+y\otimes x=(x+y)\otimes(x+y)-x\otimes x-y\otimes y$. 

^a335b3

> [!Proposition]
> Suppose that $M$ is generated by $\{ x_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda \}$. Then for all $n\geqslant 0$, $\Lambda^{n}M$ is generated by $\{ x_{\lambda_{1}}\wedge\dots \wedge x_{\lambda_{n}} :\lambda_{1}, \dots, \lambda_{n}\text{ distinct}\}$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > Follows from the fact that $\{ x_{\lambda_{1}}\wedge\dots \wedge x_{\lambda_{n}}:\lambda_{1}, \dots, \lambda_{n}\in\Lambda \}$ generates $\Lambda^{n}M$ (it is the image of a generating set of $T^{n}M$), and that if $z_{1}, \dots, z_{n}\in M$ are *not* distinct, then $z_{1}\wedge\dots \wedge z_{n}=0$. 
> 

> [!Corollary]
> If $M$ can be generated by $m$ elements (as an $R$-module), then $\Lambda^{n}M=0$ for all $n> m$.

> [!Definition] Alternating map

> The set of alternating maps $M\times\dots \times M\to N$ is denoted by $\text{Alt}_{R}(M\times\dots \times M, N)$.
