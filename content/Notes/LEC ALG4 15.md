# Exterior algebra

> [!Definition]
> Let $R$ be commutative, $M$ be an $R$-module. The **exterior algebra** $\wedge M$ of $M$ is $T(M)/J$, where $J$ is the ideal of $T(M)$ generated by $\{ x \otimes x:x\in M \}$. 

Observe that $\wedge M$ is a graded algebra, with $\wedge^{0}M\cong R$, $\wedge^{1}M\cong M$. For $n\geq 0$, $\wedge^{n}M$ is called the $n$th exterior power of $M$. The image of $x_{1}\otimes\dots \otimes x_{n}$ in $\wedge M$ is denoted by $x_{1}\wedge\dots \wedge x_{n}$. 

> [!Proposition]
> $x\wedge y=-y\wedge x$ for all $x, y\in M$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > $0=(x+y)\wedge(x+y)=x\wedge y+y\wedge x$. 
>

[[#^96459f]] is only useful when $1\ne -1$ in $R$, i.e, $2\ne 0$, i.e, $R$ is not an $\mathbb{F}_{2}$-algebra.

> If $2\ne 0$ in $R$, then $J$ can also be generated by $\{ x\otimes y+y\otimes x:x, y\in M \}$.

> [!Proposition]
> Suppose that $M$ is generated by $\{ x_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda \}$. Then for all $n\geqslant 0$, $\Lambda^{n}M$ is generated by $\{ x_{\lambda_{1}}\wedge\dots \wedge x_{\lambda_{n}} :\lambda_{1}, \dots, \lambda_{n}\text{ distinct}\}$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > Note that $\{ x_{\lambda_{1}}\wedge\dots \wedge x_{\lambda_{n}}:\lambda_{1}, \dots, \lambda_{n}\in\Lambda \}$ generates $T^{n}M$ and hence $\wedge^{n}M$. 
> > 
> > The proof proceeds as you'd expect. 
> 

> [!Corollary]
> If $M$ can be generated by $m$ elements (as an $R$-module), then $\Lambda^{n}M=0$ for all $n> m$.
