> [!Definition] Semi-locally simply connected
> A space $X$ is called **semi-locally simply connected** if every point $x\in X$ has a neighborhood $U$ with the property that loops in $U$ based at $x$ are nullhomotopic in $X$. 
> 
> Equivalently, every point $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ for which the homomorphism $\pi_{1}(U, x)\to \pi_{1}(X, x)$ induced by the inclusion $U\hookrightarrow X$ is trivial. 

^6ca9d2

Remarks:
1. The neighborhood $U$ need not be simply connected: the null homotopy is not required to take place inside of $U$. 
2. It is not required that every loop in $U$ is nullhomotopic in $X$, just the ones based at $x$. 

> [!Example]
> The [Hawaiian earring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_earring "Hawaiian earring") is not semi-locally simply connected. However, the cone of the Hawaiian earring (being contractible) is semi-locally simply connected (but not locally simply connected!).
