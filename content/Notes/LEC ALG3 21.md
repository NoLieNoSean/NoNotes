[!Theorem]
Let $|K|=q$. Then the moltiplicative group $K^{*}$ is a cyclic group of order $q-1$. 

[!Proof]-
$K^{*}$ is an abelian group under multiplication. Use the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups. 

$$
K^{*}=C_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{k}.
$$
where $|C_{1}|\ | \ |C_{2}|\ | \ \dots\ | \ |C_{n}|$. Let $C_{i}$ be generated by $v_{i}$. Take any $\alpha\in K^{*}$. We can write
$$
\alpha=a_{1}v_{1}+\dots+a_{k}v_{k}.
$$
Note that $|C_{k}|$ annihilates any $\alpha\in K^{*}$. Thus, the order of every element in $K^{*}$ divides $|C_{k}|$. Thus, every $\alpha\in K^{*}$ is a root of $x^{d}-1$ (this polynomial resides in $\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]$). 

Next, $q-1=|K^{*}|=d_{1}d_{2}\dots d_{k}$, where $d_{i}=|C_{i}|$. 

Let $f(x)=x^{q}-x$. gcd of $f$ and $f'$ is $1$, so $f$ has no multiple roots. Thus, $x^{d}-1$ is a $d$ degree polynomial with $d_{1}d_{2}\dots d_{k}$ many roots, which implies $k=1$.

---

[!Corollary]
There exists a field of order $q=p^{r}$, for $r\geqslant 1$. Any twoO such fields are isomorphic.
