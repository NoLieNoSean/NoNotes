We carry over the global environment from [[LEC ALG4 6]]. Here's the story so far:
1. $R$ is a PID, $M$ is a finitely generated $R$-module. We are after a structure theorem for such $M$. 
2. We saw that such a module can be written as a direct sum of a free module and a torsion module. 
3. We saw that a torsion module can be expressed as a direct sum of submodules annihilated by primes. 
4. Hence, to describe the structure of finitely generated modules over PIDs, we need to describe the structure of finitely generated modules annihilated by a prime $p$ - called $p$-primary modules.

# Finitely generated p-primary modules

> [!Definition] $p$-primary modules
> $M$ is a **$p$-primary module** if every element of $M$ is annihilated by some prime $p\in R$. When $M$ is Noetherian, there exists a single power $e$ that works uniformly for all $m\in M$, i.e, $p^{e}M=0$ (by the proof of [[LEC ALG4 6#^060841]]). If $e$ is such that $p^{e-1}M\ne 0$, we call $e$ the **exponent** of $M$. We define the exponent of individual elements $x\in M$ analogously and denote them by $\exp(x)$. 

> [!Remark]
> Let $M$ be an $R$-module generated by $\{ x_{1}, \dots, x_{n} \}$. Let $I$ be an $R$-ideal. Then $\overline{x}_{1}, \dots, \overline{x}_{n}\in M/IM$ generate $M/IM$ as an $R$-module. By [[LEC ALG4 3#^b14fe9]], they also generate $M/IM$ as an $R/I$-module. 

^ec621b

If $I$ is a prime ideal and $M$ is $I$-primary, the converse holds:

> [!Proposition]
> Let $M$ be a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module. Let $x_{1}, \dots, x_{m}\in M$ be such that $\overline{x}_{1}, \dots, \overline{x}_{n}\in M/pM$ generate $M/pM$ as an $R/\langle p \rangle$-vector space. Then, $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ generate $M$ as an $R$-module[^1].
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > Let $N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Rx_{i}\subseteq M$. We have to show that $N=M$. Let $y\in M$. There exist $a_{1}, \dots, a_{m}\in R$ such that $\overline{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\overline{x}_{i}$. Write $z=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}x_{i}$. Then, $y-z\in pM$. Thus, for all $y\in M$, there exists $z\in N$ such that $y-z\in pM$. We can now write
> > $$
> > \begin{array}{rll}
> > y-z & = py_{1}  & \in M \\
> > y-z-pz_{1} & =p^{2}y_{2} & \in pM \\
> > y-z-pz_{1}-p^{2}z_{2} & =p^{3}y_{3} & \in p^{2}M \\
> >  & \vdots \\
> > y-z-pz_{1} -\dots-p^{e-1}z_{e-1} & =p^{e}y_{e} & \in p^{e}M=0,
> > \end{array}
> > $$
> > where $z, z_{1}, \dots, z_{e-1}\in N$. It follows that $y\in N$. 
>

> [!Corollary]
> Every minimal generating set of a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module $M$ has the same cardinality, $\dim_{R/p}M/pM$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > Let $X_{1}$ and $X_{2}$ be two minimal generating sets for $M$, with $|X_{1}|\leqslant |X_{2}|$. By [[#^ec621b]], both $\overline{X}_{1}$ and $\overline{X}_{2}$ generate $M/pM$. Since $M/pM$ is a vector space, there exists a subset $\overline{X}_{3}\subseteq \overline{X}_{2}$ with $|\overline{X}_{3}|=|\overline{X}_{1}|$ which also generates $M/pM$. By [[#^2c9da9]], $X_{3}\subseteq X_{2}$ generates $M$, contradicting the minimality of $X_{2}$. 
>

> [!Definition]
> A subset $\{ x_{1}, \dots, x_{m} \}\subseteq M$ is called **independent** if for all $a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}$, 
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{i}x_{i}=0\implies a_{i}x_{i}=0 \,\forall i.
> $$
> 

> [!Lemma]
> Let $M$ be a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module with exponent $e$. Let $x_{1}\in M$ have exponent $e$. Let $z_{1}, \dots, z_{m}\in M/Rx_{1}$ be independent elements of $M/Rx_{1}$. Then, for all $i$ there exist lifts $y_{i}\in M$ of $z_{i}$ such that $y_{i}$ and $z_{i}$ have the same exponent. Moreover, $x_{1}, y_{2}, \dots, y_{m}$ are independent in $M$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > Let $f$ be the exponent of $z\in \{ z_{1}, \dots, z_{m} \}$. Let $y$ be a preimage of $z$. Then, $p^{f}y\in Rx_{1}$. Write $p^{f}y=cp^{g}x_{1}$, where $g\geqslant 0$ and $p\nmid c$. 
> > 1. If $g\geqslant e$, then $p^{f}y=0$. So, $\exp(y)\leqslant f$. However, $\exp(y)$ cannot be less than $f$, since $z^{\exp y}=0$. Thus, $\exp y=f$. 
> > 2. If $g< e$, we have $p^{f+e-g}y=cp^{e}x_{1}=0$ and $p^{f+e-g-1}y=cp^{e-1}x_{1}\ne 0$[^2]. Thus, $\exp y=f+e-g$. Since $\exp y\leqslant e$, we have $f\leqslant g$. Now consider $y'=y-cp^{g-f}x_{1}$, which is also a preimage of $z$. We have $p^{f}(y-cp^{g-f}x_{1})=0$. Again, $\exp y'$ cannot be less than $f$, so we have $\exp y=f$. 
> 



[!Proposition] @langAlgebra2002 3.7.5, p2
Let $M$ be a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module. Let $m$ be the cardinality of a minimal generating set of $M$. Then, there exist unique $e_{1}\geqslant e_{2}\geqslant \dots\geqslant e_{m}$ such that
$$
M\cong \frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{i}} \rangle }\oplus\dots \oplus \frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{m}} \rangle }.
$$

[!Proof]-

Note that $m$ is well defined by [[#^dc74cf]]. We induct on $m$. For $m=1$, there exists $x_{1}\in M$ which generates $M$. The $R$-module homomorphism $\varphi: R\to M$ defined by $r\mapsto rx_{1}$ is thus surjective. Let $e_{1}$ be the exponent of $x_{1}$. Clearly, $\langle p^{e_{1}} \rangle\subseteq \ker\varphi$. If $\ker\varphi=\langle a \rangle$, $a$ must divide $p^{e_{1}}$, forcing $\langle p^{e_{1}} \rangle=\ker\varphi$. Thus, 
$$
\begin{align}
M\cong \frac{R}{\ker\varphi}=\frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{1}} \rangle }. 
\end{align}
$$
Now suppose $m\geqslant 1$. Let $e_{1}$ be the exponent of $M$. Let $x_{1}\in M$ have exponent $e_{1}$. Let $\overline{M}=M/Rx_{1}$. We claim that $x_{1}\not\in pM$. 







^d3994a

> [!Example]
> Let $R=\mathbb{Z}$, $p=2$. Let $M$ be a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-module with $2^{2}M=0$. Then, there exist $m_{1}, m_{2}$ such that
> $$
> M\cong \left( \frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}} \right)^{m_{1}}\oplus\left( \frac{\mathbb{Z}}{4\mathbb{Z}} \right)^{m_{2}}.
> $$
> 



[^1]: Incidentally, the same result can be proven for finitely generated modules over local rings using Nakayama's lemma.

[^2]: Note that if $c\not\in \langle p \rangle$, $x$ and $cx$ have the same exponent. Suppose $e$ and $k$ are exponents of $x$ and $cx$ respectively. Clearly, the only possibility is $k\leqslant e$. Suppose $k< e$. Since $c$ and $p^{e-k}$ are coprime, we can write $1=\lambda c+\mu p^{e-k}$. Thus, $p^{k}x=\lambda(p^{k}cx)+\mu(p^{e}x)=0$, a contradiction.
