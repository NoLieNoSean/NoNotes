We carry over the global environment from [[LEC ALG4 6]]. Here's the story so far:
1. $R$ is a PID, $M$ is a finitely generated $R$-module. We are after a structure theorem for such $M$. 
2. We saw that such a module can be written as a direct sum of a free module and a torsion module. 
3. We saw that a torsion module can be expressed as a direct sum of submodules annihilated by primes. 
4. Hence, to describe the structure of finitely generated modules over PIDs, we need to describe the structure of finitely generated modules annihilated by a prime $p$ - called $p$-primary modules.  

---
# Finitely generated p-primary modules

> [!Definition] $p$-primary modules
> $M$ is a **$p$-primary module** if every element of $M$ is annihilated by some prime $p\in R$. When $M$ is Noetherian, there exists a single power $e$ that works uniformly for all $m\in M$, i.e, $p^{e}M=0$ (by the proof of [[LEC ALG4 6#^060841]]). If $e$ is such that $p^{e-1}M\ne 0$, we call $e$ the **exponent** of $M$. We define the exponent of individual elements $x\in M$ analogously and denote them by $\exp(x)$. 

> [!Remark]
> For $x\in M$ and $c\in R$, if $c\not\in \langle p \rangle$, $x$ and $cx$ have the same exponent. Indeed, suppose $e$ and $k$ are exponents of $x$ and $cx$ respectively. Clearly, the only possibility is $k\leqslant e$. Suppose $k< e$. Since $c$ and $p^{e-k}$ are coprime, we can write $1=\lambda c+\mu p^{e-k}$. Thus, $p^{k}x=\lambda(p^{k}cx)+\mu(p^{e}x)=0$, a contradiction. 

^212608

> [!Proposition]
> If $M$ is a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module with exponent $e$, then $\text{Ann}_{R}(M)=\langle p^{e} \rangle$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > Since $M(p)=M\ne 0$, $\text{Ann}_{R}(M)\subseteq \langle p \rangle$ by [[LEC ALG4 6#^56fd10]]. For any $q\ne p$, $M(q)\cap M(p)=M(q)\cap M=0$ by [[LEC ALG4 6#^2da73b]]. Thus, $M(q)=0$, and $\text{Ann}_{R}(M)\not\subset \langle q \rangle$. Thus, $\text{Ann}_{R}(M)$ is generated by some power of $p$. Since $p^{e}\in\text{Ann}_{R}(M)$ and $p^{e-1}\not\in\text{Ann}_{R}(M)$, we must have $\text{Ann}_{R}(M)=\langle p^{e} \rangle$. 
> 

^03412c

> [!Corollary]
> If $M$ is a singly generated $p$-primary $R$-module with exponent $e$, then $M\cong R/\langle p^{e} \rangle$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > If $M$ is singly generated by $x_{1}$, the $R$-module homomorphism $\varphi: R\to M$ defined by $r\mapsto rx_{1}$ becomes surjective. By [[#^212608]] and [[#^03412c]], $\ker\varphi=\text{Ann}_{R}(x_{1})=\text{Ann}_{R}(M)=\langle p^{e} \rangle$. Thus, 
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > M\cong \frac{R}{\ker\varphi}=\frac{R}{\langle p^{e} \rangle }. 
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> > 
> 

^015b69

> [!Remark]
> Let $M$ be an $R$-module generated by $\{ x_{1}, \dots, x_{n} \}$. Let $I$ be an $R$-ideal. Then $\overline{x}_{1}, \dots, \overline{x}_{n}\in M/IM$ generate $M/IM$ as an $R$-module. By [[LEC ALG4 3#^b14fe9]], they also generate $M/IM$ as an $R/I$-module. 

^ec621b

If $I$ is a prime ideal and $M$ is $I$-primary, the converse holds:

> [!Proposition]
> Let $M$ be a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module. Let $x_{1}, \dots, x_{m}\in M$ be such that $\overline{x}_{1}, \dots, \overline{x}_{n}\in M/pM$ generate $M/pM$ as an $R/\langle p \rangle$-vector space. Then, $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ generate $M$ as an $R$-module[^1].
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > Let $N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Rx_{i}\subseteq M$. We have to show that $N=M$. Let $y\in M$. There exist $a_{1}, \dots, a_{m}\in R$ such that $\overline{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\overline{x}_{i}$. Write $z=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}x_{i}$. Then, $y-z\in pM$. Thus, for all $y\in M$, there exists $z\in N$ such that $y-z\in pM$. We can now write
> > $$
> > \begin{array}{rll}
> > y-z & = py_{1}  & \in pM \\
> > y-z-pz_{1} & =p^{2}y_{2} & \in p^{2}M \\
> > y-z-pz_{1}-p^{2}z_{2} & =p^{3}y_{3} & \in p^{3}M \\
> >  & \vdots \\
> > y-z-pz_{1} -\dots-p^{e-1}z_{e-1} & =p^{e}y_{e} & \in p^{e}M=0,
> > \end{array}
> > $$
> > where $z, z_{1}, \dots, z_{e-1}\in N$. It follows that $y\in N$. 
> 

^2c9da9

> [!Corollary]
> Every minimal generating set of a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module $M$ has the same cardinality, $\dim_{R/p}M/pM$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > Let $X_{1}$ and $X_{2}$ be two minimal generating sets for $M$, with $|X_{1}|\leqslant |X_{2}|$. By [[#^ec621b]], both $\overline{X}_{1}$ and $\overline{X}_{2}$ generate $M/pM$. Since $M/pM$ is a vector space, there exists a subset $\overline{X}_{3}\subseteq \overline{X}_{2}$ with $|\overline{X}_{3}|=|\overline{X}_{1}|$ which also generates $M/pM$. By [[#^2c9da9]], $X_{3}\subseteq X_{2}$ generates $M$, contradicting the minimality of $X_{2}$. 
> 

^dc74cf

> [!Definition]
> A subset $\{ x_{1}, \dots, x_{m} \}\subseteq M$ is called **independent** if for all $a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}$, 
> $$
> \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{i}x_{i}=0\implies a_{i}x_{i}=0 \,\forall i.
> $$
> 

^8f3734

> [!Remark]
> $y_{1}, \dots, y_{m}$ are independent iff the module $\langle y_{1}, \dots, y_{m} \rangle$ has the direct sum decomposition
> $$
> \langle y_{1}, \dots, y_{m} \rangle =\langle y_{1} \rangle \oplus\dots \oplus \langle y_{m} \rangle
> $$
> in terms of the cyclic modules $\langle y_{i} \rangle$. 

^82f2ff

> [!Lemma]
> Let $M$ be a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module with exponent $e$. Let $x_{1}\in M$ have exponent $e$. Let $z_{1}, \dots, z_{m}\in M/Rx_{1}$ be independent elements of $M/Rx_{1}$. Then, for all $i$ there exist lifts $y_{i}\in M$ of $z_{i}$ such that $y_{i}$ and $z_{i}$ have the same exponent. Moreover, $x_{1}, y_{1}, \dots, y_{m}$ are independent in $M$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > Let $f$ be the exponent of $z\in \{ z_{1}, \dots, z_{m} \}$. Let $y$ be a preimage of $z$. Then, $p^{f}y\in Rx_{1}$. Write $p^{f}y=cp^{g}x_{1}$, where $g\geqslant 0$ and $p\nmid c$. 
> > 1. If $g\geqslant e$, then $p^{f}y=0$. So, $\exp(y)\leqslant f$. However, $\exp(y)$ cannot be less than $f$, since $z^{\exp y}=0$. Thus, $\exp y=f$. 
> > 2. If $g< e$, we have $p^{f+e-g}y=cp^{e}x_{1}=0$ and, by [[#^212608]], $p^{f+e-g-1}y=cp^{e-1}x_{1}\ne 0$. Thus, $\exp y=f+e-g$. Since $\exp y\leqslant e$, we have $f\leqslant g$. Now consider $y'=y-cp^{g-f}x_{1}$, which is also a preimage of $z$. We have $p^{f}(y-cp^{g-f}x_{1})=0$. Again, $\exp y'$ cannot be less than $f$, so we have $\exp y=f$. 
> 

^7cea38

> [!Lemma]
> Let $p, b\in R$ with $p$ prime. Then, $R/\langle p \rangle\cong bR/\langle pb \rangle$. 

^743215

> [!Theorem] @langAlgebra2002 3.7.5, p2
> Let $M$ be a finitely generated $p$-primary $R$-module. Let $s=\dim_{R/p}M/pM$. Then, there exist ==unique== $e_{1}\geqslant e_{2}\geqslant \dots\geqslant e_{s}$ such that
> $$
> M\cong \frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{1}} \rangle }\oplus\dots \oplus \frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{s}} \rangle }.
> $$
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > $s$ is the cardinality of any minimal generating of $M$ by [[#^dc74cf]]. We induct on $s$. For $s=1$, we are done by [[#^015b69]]. 
> > 
> > Now suppose $s\geqslant 1$. Let $e_{1}$ be the exponent of $M$. Let $x_{1}\in M$ have exponent $e_{1}$. Denote $M/pM$ by $M_{p}$. Clearly $x_{1}\not\in pM$, since if $x_{1}=pm$, we have a contradiction by $p^{e-1}x_{1}=p^{e}m=0$. Now, consider the image of $\tilde{x}_{1}$ under the projection $M\twoheadrightarrow M_{p}$. Since $M_{p}$ is a $R/p$-vector space of dimension $s$, we can choose $\tilde{y}_{2}, \dots, \tilde{y}_{s}\in M_{p}$ such that $\{ \tilde{x}_{1}, \tilde{y}_{2}, \dots, \tilde{y}_{s} \}$ is a $R/p$-basis for $M_{p}$. It follows from [[#^2c9da9]] that $x_{1}, y_{2}, \dots, y_{s}$ generate $M$ as an $R$-module. 
> > 
> > Next, consider consider the projection $M\twoheadrightarrow M/x_{1}R$. Note that $M/x_{1}R$ is generated by the $s-1$ generators $\overline{y}_{2}, \dots, \overline{y}_{s}$. Using the induction hypothesis, we can write
> > $$
> > \frac{M}{x_{1}R}= \frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{2}} \rangle }\oplus\dots \oplus \frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{s}} \rangle },
> > $$
> > with $e_{2}\geqslant \dots\geqslant e_{s}$. Let $\overline{z}_{2}, \dots, \overline{z}_{s}\in M/x_{1}R$ be generators of the $s$ components of the above direct sum with exponents $e_{2}, .., e_{s}$ respectively. They are necessarily independent. Use [[#^7cea38]] to lift these to $z_{2}, \dots, z_{s}\in M$ such that $x_{1}, z_{2}, \dots, z_{s}$ are independent and have exponents $e_{1}\geqslant e_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant e_{s}$ (recall that $e_{1}$ is the highest exponent an element of $M$ can have). 
> > 
> > These generate $M$: for $m\in M$, we have
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > m+x_{1}R & =\sum_{i=2}^{s} \alpha_{i}(z_{i}+x_{1}R ) \\
> >  & =\sum_{i=2}^{s} \alpha_{i}z_{i}+x_{1}R \\
> > \implies m & =\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{s} \alpha_{i}z_{i}.
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> > Thus, by [[#^82f2ff]], we have
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > M=\frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{1}} \rangle }\oplus\dots \oplus\frac{R}{\langle p^{e_{s}} \rangle }. 
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> > 
> > ---
> > 
> > We now prove uniqueness. Consider the submodules $pM, p^{2}M, \dots, p^{e_{1}-1}M$ of $M$. Let $n_{k}$ denote the number of $e_{i}$ for which $e_{i}> k$. Let $e_{i_{k}}$ be the smallest $e_{i}$ greater than $k$. Observe that the direct sum
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > p^{k}M=p^{k}\frac{R}{ p^{e_{1}}R  }\oplus\dots \oplus p^{k}\frac{R}{ p^{e_{s}}R  }=p^{k}\frac{R}{ p^{e_{1}}R  }\oplus\dots \oplus p^{k}\frac{R}{ p^{e_{i_{k}}}R  }
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> > has exactly $n_{k}$ nontrivial summands, since $p^{k}R/ p^{e_{i}}R$ is $0$ for all $k\geqslant e_{i}$. Now consider the quotient $p^{k}M/p^{k+1}M$. If $n_{k}=n_{k+1}$, we have
> > $$
> > \frac{p^{k}M}{p^{k+1}M}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\left( \frac{\frac{p^{k}R}{ p^{e_{i}}R }}{\frac{p^{k+1}R}{ p^{e_{i}}R }} \right)\cong\bigoplus_{n_{k}}\frac{R}{pR}
> > $$
> > by the [[LEC ALG3 3#^7c2d63|third isomorphism theorem]] and [[#^743215]]. It is easy to show that even in the case $n_{k}> n_{k+1}$, the above isomorphism continues to hold. Thus, 
> > $$
> > \dim_{R/p}\frac{p^{k}M}{p^{k+1}M}=n_{k}.
> > $$
> > The dimensions of these quotient vector spaces are an invariant of $M$. Since the sequence $e_{1}, \dots, e_{s}$ is completely determined by $n_{0}, \dots, n_{e_{1}-1}$, we conclude that our decomposition is unique. 
> 

^d3994a

> [!Example]
> Let $R=\mathbb{Z}$, $p=2$. Let $M$ be a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-module with $2^{2}M=0$. Then, there exist $m_{1}, m_{2}$ such that
> $$
> M\cong \left( \frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}} \right)^{m_{1}}\oplus\left( \frac{\mathbb{Z}}{4\mathbb{Z}} \right)^{m_{2}}.
> $$
> 



[^1]: Incidentally, the same result can be proven for finitely generated modules over local rings using Nakayama's lemma.
