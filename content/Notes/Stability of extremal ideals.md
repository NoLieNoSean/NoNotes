# [[Associated primes of monomial ideals|Preliminaries: Associated primes of monomial ideals]]

# Extremal ideals

> [!Definition]
> $S_{q}:=k[y_{A}:A\subseteq[q]]$. 
> $\mathcal{E}_{q}:=(\varepsilon_{1}, \dots, \varepsilon_{q})\subseteq S_{q}$, where 
> $$
> \varepsilon_{i}=\prod_{A\subseteq[q], i\in A}y_{A}.
> $$

```Macaulay2
Extremal= q-> (
    S=set {1};
    for i from 1 to q  do S=S+set{i};
    PS=subsets S;
    V={};
    for i from 1 to #PS-1 do V=V|{x_{PS#i}};
    R=QQ[V];
    Elist={};
    for i from 1 to q do (
	    e_i=1;
	    for j from 0 to #PS-1 do  if isSubset(set{i},PS#j) then
	        e_i=e_i*x_{PS#j};
        Elist=Elist|{e_i};
    );
    return monomialIdeal(Elist);
)
```

For example,
$$
\mathcal{E}_{3}=(y_{1}y_{12}y_{13}y_{123}, y_{2}y_{12}y_{23}y_{123}, y_{3}y_{13}y_{23}y_{123}).
$$
Let $I$ be a squarefree monomial ideal with $q$ generators. Then, properties of $I$ can be gleaned from properties of $\mathcal{E}_{q}$. Here's an example:

> [!Theorem]
> $\text{astab}(I)\leq\text{astab}(\mathcal{E}_{q})$, where $I$ is a squarefree monomial ideal with $q$ generators.

Our goal is to find $\text{astab}(\mathcal{E}_{q})$ for all $q$.
## Tool to find non-associated primes of $\mathcal{E}_{q}^{r}$

Let $I$ be a monomial ideal. By [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^3ae2fa]], every $P\in\text{Ass}(I)$ is a monomial prime ideal, that is, is generated by single powers of variables. Suppose $P=(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})\in\text{Ass}(I)$. Then, by [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^676130]], $P=(I:f)$ for some monomial $f$, and thus, by [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^03f88c]], 
$$
f\in(I:P)\setminus I=\left( \bigcap_{i=1}^{n} (I:x_{i}) \right) \setminus I.
$$
(Note that $f\not\in I$, since if it was, $I:f$ would be the entire ring). Hence we have the following lemma:

> [!Lemma]
> Let $I$ be a monomial ideal and $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ be some variables. If $I:(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})=I$, then $(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})\not\in\text{Ass}(I)$.

^2e6cc7

Note that for ideals $A\subseteq B$, we have $(I:B)\subseteq(I:A)$, since $rB\subseteq I$ implies $rA\subseteq I$ for $r\in R$. Let $P:=(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})$ be as in the above lemma. If $J$ is a prime ideal such that $P\subseteq J$, we have
$$
(I:J)\subseteq(I:P)=I.
$$
however, $I\subseteq(I:J)$ always holds, so $(I:J)=I$, and by [[#^2e6cc7]], $J\not\in\text{Ass}(I)$.

> [!Theorem]
> Let $I$ be a monomial ideal and $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ be some variables. If $I:(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})=I$, then no associated prime of $I$ contains $(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})$.

^7d15f2

## General observations

$\mathcal{E}_{q}$ is a squarefree monomial ideal. There are finitely many monomial prime ideals in $S_{q}$. Precisely, $S_{q}$ has $2^{2^{q}-1}-1$ prime monomial ideals. By [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^3ae2fa]], all associated primes of $\mathcal{E}_{q}$ are monomial prime ideals. 

[[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^84e161]] tells us that $\mathcal{E}_{q}$ is the intersection of the ideals in $\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{q})$. When this is regarded as a primary decomposition of $\mathcal{E}_{q}$, [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^53e841]] tells us that $\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}_{q})=\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{q})$ for all $q$.

> [!proposition]
> $\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}_{q})=\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{q})$ for all $q$.

By [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^d3a486]], the associated primes of higher powers of $\mathcal{E}_{q}$ contain $\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{q})$, and maybe additional embedded primes from the pool of monomial prime ideals.

## Finding $\text{astab}(\mathcal{E}_{2})$

$\mathcal{E}_{2}=(y_{1}y_{12}, y_{2}y_{12})$. It is easily seen that $\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{2})=\{ (y_{1}, y_{2}), (y_{12}) \}$ by [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^5f7307]] and [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^716dce]]. We can say more:

> [!Claim]
> $\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{r})=\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{2})$ for all $r\geq 1$. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > It follows from [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^d3a486]] that $\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\subseteq\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{r})$. By [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^3ae2fa]], we only need to consider the monomial prime ideals of $k[y_{1}, y_{2}, y_{12}]$ for members of $\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{r})$. The ones not already in $\text{Min}(\mathcal{E}_{2})$ are
> > $$
> > (y_{1}), (y_{2}), (y_{1}, y_{12}), (y_{2}, y_{12}), (y_{1}, y_{2}, y_{12}).
> > $$
> > Using [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^03f88c]] and [[Associated primes of monomial ideals#^3f2f38]], we have
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > \mathcal{E}^{r}_{2}:(y_{2}, y_{12}) & =(\mathcal{E}^{r}_{2}:(y_{2}))\cap(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{r}:(y_{12})) \\
> >  & =(y_{1}^{r}y_{12}^{r}, y_{2}^{r-1}y_{12}^{r})\cap(y_{1}^{r}y_{12}^{r-1}, y_{2}^{r}y_{12}^{r-1}) \\
> >  & = (y_{1}^{r}y_{12}^{r}, y_{2}^{r}y_{12}^{r}) \\
> >  & =\mathcal{E}_{2}^{r}.
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> > Thus, by [[#^7d15f2]], $(y_{2}, y_{12})$ and $(y_{1}, y_{2}, y_{12})$ are not in $\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}^{r}_{2})$. By a symmetric argument, $(y_{1}, y_{12})$ is not in $\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}^{r}_{2})$. As for $(y_{1})$ and $(y_{2})$, they are contained in the minimal prime $(y_{1}, y_{2})$, and hence are not in $\text{Ass}(\mathcal{E}^{r}_{2})$[^1]. The claim follows.
