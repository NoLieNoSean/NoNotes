Recall that an isometry is an invertible map between metric spaces that preserves the metric. A isometry of Euclidean spaces is also called a **rigid motion**. 

> [!Definition] Geometric manifold
> A **geometric manifold** is a [[Topological manifolds|topological manifold]] $M$ with an atlas that satisfies the additional requirement that the transition maps are restrictions of isometries in a given geometry. 

> [!Definition] Morphisms in GEOM, @schultensIntroduction3manifolds2014 1.5.5
> Given two geometric manifolds $(M, \phi_{\alpha})$, $(N, \psi_{\beta})$, a map $f:M\to N$ is an **isometry** if for all $\alpha, \beta$ the maps $\phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\circ f\circ \psi_{\beta}$ are restrictions of isometries in the given geometry. 

#q @schultensIntroduction3manifolds2014 1.5.7 says connected Euclidean manifolds have a well-defined metric. How?

> [!Proposition] #q
> 1. $\mathbb{T}^{n}$ is a Euclidean manifold. 
> 2. $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ is a Euclidean manifold. 
> 3. $\mathbb{S}^{n}$ is not a Euclidean manifold for $n> 1$.
