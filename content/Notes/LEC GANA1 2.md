# Algebras and monotone classes

Let $\Omega$ denote a general set. Symbols denoted by script letters such at $\mathcal{A}$ represent classes of subsets of $\Omega$. 

> [!Definition] Algebra
> A family $\mathcal{A}$ is said to be an **algebra** if
> 1. $\Omega\in \mathcal{A}$;
> 2. $e\in \mathcal{A}\implies E^{c}\in \mathcal{A}$; 
> 3. $E, F\in A\implies E\cup F\in \mathcal{A}$. 

$\sigma$-algebras are algebras that are closed under countable unions:

> [!Definition] $\sigma$-algebra
> A family $\mathcal{B}$ is called a **$\sigma$-algebra** if 
> 1. $\Omega\in \mathcal{B}$; 
> 2. $E\in \mathcal{B}\implies E^{c}\in \mathcal{B}$; 
> 3. If $\{ E_{n} \}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq \mathcal{B}$, then $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\in \mathcal{B}$. 

Being closed under complements and countable unions implies being closed under countable intersections.

> [!Proposition]
> If $\mathcal{S}$ is any collection of subsets of $\Omega$, then there exists a smallest $\sigma$-algebra of subsets of $\Omega$ which contains $\mathcal{S}$, given by 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \bigcap \{ \mathcal{B}:\mathcal{B}\text{ is a }\sigma\text{-algebra and }\mathcal{S}\subseteq \mathcal{B}  \}.
> \end{align}
> $$
> We call this minimal $\sigma$-algebra the **$\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal{S}$** and denote it by $\sigma(\mathcal{S})$.

A monotone class is a family closed under monotone limits:

> [!Definition] Monotone class
> A family $\mathcal{M}$ is said to be a **monotone class** provided:
> 1. If $E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\dots$ and $E_{n}\in \mathcal{M}$ $\forall n$, then $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\in \mathcal{M}$; and
> 2. If $E_{1}\supseteq E_{2}\supseteq\dots$ and $E_{n}\in \mathcal{M}$ $\forall n$, then $\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\in \mathcal{M}$. 

Given a family $\mathcal{S}$, the **monotone class generated by $\mathcal{S}$** is defined analogously to [[#^bfc1ae]] and denoted by $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{S})$. 

> [!Proposition] Monotone class lemma, @athreyaMeasureProbability2018 Prp 1.3.2
> If $\mathcal{A}$ is an algebra of subsets of $\Omega$, then $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})=\sigma(\mathcal{A})$.

# Measures

> [!Definition] Measure, @athreyaMeasureProbability2018 1.4.4
> Let $\mathcal{A}\subseteq P(\Omega)$ be an algebra, and $\mu:\mathcal{A}\to[0, \infty]$. 
> 1. $\mu$ is said to be **finitely additive** if $A, B\in \mathcal{A}$, $A\cap B=\emptyset$ $\implies$ $\mu(A\cup B)=\mu(A)+\mu(B)$. 
> 2. $\mu$ is said to be **countably additive** if it satisfies the following criterion: If $\{ A_{n} \}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is a countable collection of sets in $\mathcal{A}$ such that (i) $A_{n}\cap A_{m}=\emptyset$ for $n\ne m$, and (ii) $A=\coprod_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in \mathcal{A}$, then $\mu(A)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{n})$. 
> 3. $\mu$ is called a **measure** if $\mu(\emptyset)=0$ and $\mu$ is countably additive. 
> 4. A measure $\mu$ is said to be **finite** if $\mu(\Omega)< \infty$. 
> 5. A measure $\mu$ is said to be **$\sigma$-finite** if there exist sets $\{ A_{n} \}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq \mathcal{A}$ with $\mu(A_{n})< \infty$ for all $n\in \mathbb{N}$ which satisfy $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}=\Omega$. 


[!Proposition]
Suppose $\mu$ is finitely additive. Then, $\mu$ is $\sigma$-additive iff $\mu$ is monotone continuous (if $A_{n}\uparrow A\in \mathcal{A}$ then $\mu(A_{n})\uparrow \mu(A)$).

[!Proposition]
Let $\mu:\mathcal{A}\to[0, \infty]$. Suppose $\mu(\emptyset)=0$. Then, $\mu$ is a measure iff $\mu$ is finitely additive and countably subadditive. 

[!Proof]-

$(\implies)$ is clear. 

$(\impliedby)$ Suppose $\{ A_{n} \}\subseteq \mathcal{A}$ are pairwise disjoint. By subadditivity, we have
$$
\mu\left( \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_{n} \right)\leqslant \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (A_{n}).
$$
Using finite additivity (and taking a limit), we have the opposite inequality.

[!Definition] limsup and liminf
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a $\sigma$-algebra. Let $\mu$ be a measure on $\mathcal{F}$. Let $\{ A_{n} \}\subseteq \mathcal{F}$. Define
$$
\limsup A_{n}:=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigcup_{m=n}^{\infty} A_{m}
$$
$$
\liminf A_{n}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigcap_{m=n}^{\infty} A_{m}.
$$


Clearly, $\lim\inf A_{n}\subseteq \limsup A_{n}$.

[!Proposition]
$\mu$ is a finite measure on $\mathcal{F}$. Then
$$
\mu(\liminf A_{n})\leqslant \liminf\mu(A_{n})\leqslant \limsup\mu(A_{n})\leqslant \mu(\limsup(A_{n})).
$$


[!Proposition] Borel-Cantelli
Let $\mu$ be a finite measure on $\mathcal{F}$. Suppose $\{ A_{n} \}\subseteq \mathcal{F}$ such that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{n})< \infty$. Then $\mu(\limsup(A_{n}))=0$. 

[!Proof]-

Let $B_{n}=\bigcup_{m=n}^{\infty}A_{m}$. 
$\mu(B_{n})\leqslant \sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\mu(A_{m})\to 0$ as $n\to \infty$ (use summability hypothesis). 
Thus, $\mu(\limsup A_{n})=\inf \mu(B_{n})=0$.

[!Proposition] Caratheodory extension
$\mu$ is a measure on an algebra $\mathcal{A}$. Then $\mu$ extends to a measure $\tilde{\mu}$ on $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$. If $\mu$ is $\sigma$-finite, then this extension is unique.
