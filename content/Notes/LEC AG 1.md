Hartshone (ch 1) and Mumford's red book

Suppose $k$ is an algebraically closed field. 


[!Theorem] Noether Normalization Lemma
Let $R$ be an integral domain, finitely generated over a field $k$. If $R$ has transcendence degree $n$ over $k$, then there exist $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}\in R$ which are algebraically independent over $k$ such that $R$ is integrally dependent on the subring $k[x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}]$ generated by the $x_{i}$s. 

[!Proof]-

Write $R=k[Y_{1}, \dots, Y_{m}]/p$, where $p$ is a prime ideal, since $R$ is finitely generated over $k$. If $m=n$, setting $y_{i}$ as the image of $Y_{i}(\text{mod }p)$, $\{ y_{1}, \dots, y_{m} \}$ are algebraically independent. Then $p=(0)$. Letting $x_{i}=y_{i}$, the lemma follows. 

Suppose $m>n$. We induct on $m$. It suffices to find a subring $S$ in $R$ generated by $m-1$ elements and such that $R$ is integrally dependent on $S$ (transitivity of integral dependence!).

Since $m> n$, $\{ y_{1}, \dots, y_{n} \}$ cannot be algebraically independent over $k$. Let $f\in k[Y_{1}, \dots, Y_{m}]$ be a polynomial such that $f(y_{1}, \dots, ,y_{m})=0$ in $R$. 

Let $r_{2}, \dots, r_{m}$ be positive integers and let
$$
\begin{align}
z_{2}:=y_{2}-y_{1}^{r_{2}}, z_{3}:=y_{3}-y_{1}^{r_{3}}, \dots, z_{m}=y_{m}-y_{1}^{r_{m}}.
\end{align}
$$
Then $f(y_{1}, z_{2}+y_{1}^{r_{1}}, \dots, z_{m}+y_{1}^{r_{m}})=0$, which implies $(y_{1}, z_{2}, \dots, z_{m})$ is a root of $f(Y_{1}, Z_{2}+Y_{1}^{r_{2}}, \dots, Z_{m}+Y_{1}^{r_{m}})$. 

A term $u\cdot \prod_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}^{b_{i}}$ of $f$ gives rise to various terms in the new polynomial, including a monomial term $u\cdot Y_{1}^{b_{1}+b_{2}r_{2}+\dots+b_{m}r_{m}}$. We can choose $r_{1}, \dots, r_{m}$ such that these monomials have different degrees for different terms. 

Therefore, $f(Y_{1}, Z_{2}+Y_{1}^{r_{2}}, \dots, Z_{m}+Y_{1}^{r_{m}})=aY_{1}^{N}+\text{terms of degree less than }N$. Thus, this polynomial servers as a relation of algebraic dependence of $Y_{1}$ over the ring $k[Z_{2}, \dots, Z_{m}]$. $Y_{2}, \dots, Y_{m}$ are also algebraically dependent on this ring. This completes the induction.
