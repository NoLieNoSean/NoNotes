$R$: commutative ring with $1$. 

> [!Exercise]
> Let $M$ be an $R$-module. An abelian subgroup $N$ of $M$ is a submodule $\iff$ the inclusion $N\hookrightarrow M$ is $R$-linear. 

> [!Exercise]
> Let $\varphi:M\to N$ be a homomorphism. Then $\varphi$ is an isomorphism $\iff$ $\varphi$ is bijective.

Every vector space (over a field) has a basis. Not true for arbitrary rings. 

Example: Let $R=k[x, y]$. $I=(x, y)$ is an $R$-module. $I$ does not have a $R$-basis: 
1. $\{ x, y \}$ clearly generates $I$, by is not linearly independent: $xy-yx=0$.
2. Any subset of $\{ x, y \}$ does not generate $I$; in fact no singleton can generate $I$. 
3. Repeat $(1)$ for any subset $S\subseteq I$ with $|S|\geqslant 2$. 

> [!Definition] Free module
> An $R$-module $M$ is said to be **free** if it has a basis.
