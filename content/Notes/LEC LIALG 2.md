[!Example]
Let $A(n, F)$ be the set of all$n\times n$ upper triangular matrices with entries in $F$. Then $A(n, F)$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}(n, F)$. 

Let $\eta(n, F)$ be the set of all $n\times n$ strictly upper triangular metrices with entries in $F$. THen $\eta(n, F)$ is a Lie subalgebra of $A(n, F)$. 

Let $d(n, F)$ be the set of all $n\times n$ diagonal matrices with entries in $F$. Then $d(n, F)$ is a Lie subalgebra of $A(n, F)$. Further, $A(n, F)=\eta(n, F)\oplus d(n, F)$.

[!Definition]
Let $A$ be an $F$-algebra (not necessarily associative). An $F$-linear map $\delta:A\to A$ is said to be an $F$-derivative of $A$ if $\delta(ab)=a\delta(b)+\delta(a)b$ for all $a, b\in A$.

[!Definition]
Let $L$ be a Lie algebra over $F$. Let $V$ be a finite dimensional vector space over $F$. A homomorphism $\varphi:L\to \mathfrak{gl}(V)$ is called a **representation** of $L$. That is, $\varphi$ is $F$-linear and
$$
\varphi([xy])=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)-\varphi(y)\varphi(x)\quad \forall x, y\in L.
$$

[!Example]
Let $L$ be a Lie algebra over $F$. 

[!Claim]
$\text{ad}:L\to \mathfrak{gl}(V)$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism. This representation is called the adjoint representation of $L$.

[!Definition]
A Lie algebra $L$ over $F$ is said to be abelian if $[xy]=0$ for all $x, y\in L$. 

Every one dimensional Lie algebra is abelian. 

Example: 
$$
L=\{ (a_{ij})\in \mathcal{gl}(n, F):a_{ij}=0\text{ if either }i\ne 1 \text{ or }j=1\}
$$

[!Definition]
Let $L$ be a Lie algebra over $F$. A vector subspace $I$ of $L$ is said to be an ideal of $L$ if $[xy]\in I$ for all $x\in L$ and $y\in I$. 

Note that a vector subspace $I\subseteq L$ is an ideal of $L$ if and only if $[xy]\in L$ for all $x\in I$ and $y\in L$, since $[xy]=-[xy]$. 

Check that $\eta(n, F)$ is an ideal of $A(n, F)$.

[!Definition]
Let $L$ be a Lie algebra of $F$. The center of $L$ is defined as 
$$
Z(L):=\{ x\in L:[xy]:[xz]=0 \forall x\in L \}.
$$
Clearly, $Z(L)$ is an ideal of $L$. 

[!Example]
$L=\mathfrak{gl}(n, F)$. Then $Z(L)$ is the set of all $n\times n$ scalar matrices with entries in $F$. 

Note that $L$ is abelian iff $Z(L)=L$.

[!Definition]
Let m$L$ be a Lie algebra over $F$. Let $I$ and $J$ be two ideals of $L$. Then the sum $I+J$ is defined as $I+J=\{ x+y:x\in I, y\in J \}$. 

Clearly, $I+J$ is a vector subspace of $L$. 

[!Claim]
$I+J$ is an ideal of $L$: If $x\in L$, $y\in I$ and $z\in J$, then $[x(y+z)]=[xy]=[xz]$.

[!Definition]
Let $L$ be a Lie algebra over $F$. Denote by $[LL]$ the smallest vector subspace of $L$ generated by elements of the form $[xy]$ for $x, y\in L$. 

Note that $[LL]$ is an ideal of $L$. 

Similar definition for $[IJ]$ can be made where $I, J$ are ideals of $L$.
