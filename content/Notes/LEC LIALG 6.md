Recall [[LEC LIALG 5#^684123]]. 

> [!Theorem] Engel
> Let $L$ be a Lie algebra. If all elements of $L$ are $\text{ad}$-nilpotent, then $L$ is nilpotent. 
> 
> > [!Proof]-
> > 
> > If $L=0$, the result is straight forward. So assume $L\ne 0$. Now, consider the adjoint representation of $L$. 
> > $$
> > \text{ad}_{L}:L\to \mathfrak{gl}(L)
> > $$
> > is a Lie algebra homomorphism. 
> > 
> > By hypothesis, $\text{ad}_{L}$ is a nilpotent endomorphism of $L$ for every $x\in L$. Hence, $\text{ad}_{L}(L)$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}(L)$ consisting of nilpotent elements. By [[LEC LIALG 5#^684123]], there is a nonzero element $z\in L$ such that $\text{ad}_{L}(x)(z)=0$ for all $x\in L$. Hence $Z(L)\ne 0$. 
> > 
> > Let $L'=L/Z(L)$. For any $x, y\in L$, we have
> > $$
> > \text{ad}_{L'}(x+Z(L))(y+Z(L))=(\text{ad}_{L}(x)(y)+Z(L)).
> > $$
> > Since $\text{ad}_{L}x$ is nilpotent for every $x\in L$, $\text{ad}_{L'}(x+Z(L))$ is nilpotent for every $x\in L$. 
> > 
> > Further, we have $\dim(L')\leqslant \dim(L)-1$. Therefore, by induction on $\dim L$, $L'$ is nilpotent. But $L'=L/Z(L)$. Therefore, by @humphreysIntroductionLieAlgebras1972 [p. 12], $L$ is nilpotent. 
>
