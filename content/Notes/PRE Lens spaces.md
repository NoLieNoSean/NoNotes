# Redundancy in notation

Dehn twists about the meridional curve extend to homeomorphisms of $\mathbb{T}^{2}$. 
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

<iframe src="https://www.desmos.com/3d/w30xpm9iih" width="100%" height= "500px"></iframe>

*Dehn twists about the longitudinal curve do not*! For example, if the Dehn twist $m\mapsto m+l$ extended to a homeomorphism $h$ of $\mathbb{B}^{2}\times \mathbb{S}^{1}$, then $e=h_{*}(m)=m+l\ne e$!


---


So, given $\begin{bmatrix}q & s \\ p & r\end{bmatrix}\in\text{Mod}(\mathbb{T}^{2})$, we can precompose and postcompose by Dehn twists without altering the lens space. 
$$
\begin{align}
\begin{bmatrix}q & s \\ p & r\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
1 & k \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}  & = \begin{bmatrix}q & s+qk \\ p & r+pk\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & k \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}q & s \\ p & r\end{bmatrix}  & = \begin{bmatrix}q+pk & s+rk \\ p & r\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}
$$
These manifest as the following matrix operations:
- Adding a multiple of the second row to the first row
- Adding a multiple of the first column to the second column

Now, consider the lens space determined by $\begin{bmatrix}q & s \\ p & r\end{bmatrix}\in\text{Mod}(\mathbb{T}^{2})$. Let $T=\partial T_{1}=\partial T_{2}$. By The $\epsilon$-neighborhood theorem, we can consider a regular neighborhood $N$ of $T$ in the total space. Let
$$
\begin{align}
U & = N\cup T_{1}, \\
V  & = N\cup T_{2},  \\
U\cap V & =N.
\end{align}
$$
$N\cong \mathbb{T}^{2}\times I$ has fundamental group isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^{2}$. Denote the generators of $N$ by $\{ l, m \}$. Both $U$ and $V$ have fundamental groups isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $l_{1}$ and $l_{2}$ respectively ($m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ are nullhomotopic in $U$ and $V$). 

WLOG, we can assume $i_{*}(m)=m_{1}$ and $i_{*}(l)=l_{1}$.

We can now compute the relations in the amalgamated free product:
$$
\begin{align}
e=m_{1}  & = m_{2}^{q}l_{2}^{p}=l_{2}^{p}, \\
l_{1} & =m_{2}^{s}l_{2}^{r}=l_{2}^{r}.
\end{align}
$$
Thus, 
$$
\pi_{1}(L(p, q))=\langle l_{1}, l_{2}\ | \  l_{2}^{p}=e, l_{1}=l_{2}^{r} \rangle =\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.
$$

# L(1, 0)

Consider $\begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\in\text{Mod}(\mathbb{T}^{2})$. To begin, observe that this is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism. However, boundary identifications must be orientation reversing. We must change our perspective!

# Trick: Move *into* one of the spaces

We're now inside $T_{1}$! We've reoriented the surface closest to us to have normals pointing outwards. Notice how this makes the boundary identification orientation preserving.
