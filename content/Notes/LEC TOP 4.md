Counterexamples for bijective continuous $\implies$ homeomorphism. 

1. Identity map from a finer to coarser topology
2. $[0, 2\pi)\xmapsto{f}S^{1}\subseteq \mathbb{R}^{2}$ by $t\mapsto(\cos t, \sin t)$. $f$ is bijective. $f$ is continuous:
	1. $[0, 2\pi)\hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}\xrightarrow{\cos}\mathbb{R}$ is continuous, as is $\sin$. 
	2. $[0, 2\pi)\xrightarrow{\cos \times \sin}\mathbb{R}\times \mathbb{R}$ is continuous by the way product topology on $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ is defined. 
   
   However, $f([0, \pi))$ is not open. 

> [!Definition]
> A map $X\xrightarrow i Y$ such that $X\xrightarrow{i}i(X)$ is a homeomorphism is called an **embedding**.
