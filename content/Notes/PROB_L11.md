> [!Definition]
> A sequence $X_{1}, X_{2}, \dots$ of random variables with distribution functions $F_{1}, F_{2}, \dots$ is said to **converge in distribution** to a random variable $X$ with distribution function $F$ , denoted by $X_{n}\xrightarrow{d}X$, if
> $$
> \lim_{ n \to \infty } F_{n}(x)=F(x)
> $$
> for all $x\in \mathbb{R}$ at which $F$ is continuous. 

> [!Theorem]
> Let $X_{1}, X_{2},\dots$ and $X$ be continuous random variables. If $\varphi_{X_{n}}$ converges to $\varphi_{X}$ pointwise, then $X_{n}\xrightarrow{d}X$.
