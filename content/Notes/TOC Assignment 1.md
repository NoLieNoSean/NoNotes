# Problem 2

> [!Question]
> Let $\Sigma$ be a finite alphabet. Given a language $L$, define
> $$
> \text{yolk}(L):=\{ y: \exists z, x\in\Sigma^{*} \text{ such that }|x|=|y|=|z| \text{ and }xxyzz\in L \}.
> $$
> Show that if $L$ is recognizable, $\text{yolk}(L)$ is recognizable. 

Let $M=(Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_{0}, F)$ be a DFA recognizing $L$. For $a\in\Sigma$, let $\sigma_{a}:Q\to Q$ be the map $q\mapsto\delta(q, a)$. For $w=a_{1}\dots a_{k}\in\Sigma^{*}$ (where $a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}\in\Sigma$), let $\sigma'_{w}:Q\to Q$ be the map $q\mapsto(\sigma_{a_{k}}\circ\dots \circ\sigma_{a_{1}})(q)$. 

Define an automaton $\mathbf{M}=(\mathbf{Q}, \Sigma, \boldsymbol{\delta}, \mathbf{q}_{0}, \mathbf{F})$ where
$$
\begin{align}
 & \mathbf{Q}:=\{ (S, \varphi) : S\subseteq Q^{Q}, \varphi\in Q^{Q} \}, \\
 & \boldsymbol{\delta}((S, \varphi), a):=(\{ \sigma_{b}\circ\psi:\psi\in S, b\in\Sigma \}, \sigma_{a}\circ\varphi), \\
 & \mathbf{q}_{0}:=(\{ \sigma_{\epsilon} \}, \sigma_{\epsilon}), \\
 & \mathbf{F}:=\{ (S, \varphi) : \exists (\alpha, \beta \in S ,\,\,q_{f}\in F) \text{ such that }  (\alpha \circ\alpha \circ\varphi \circ\beta \circ\beta)(q_{0})=q_{f}\}.
\end{align}
$$
$\sigma_{\epsilon}$ denotes the identity on $Q$. 

> [!Claim]
> $\mathbf{M}$ recognizes $\text{yolk(L)}$.
> 
> > [!Proof]
> > Let $y\in \text{yolk}(L)$. Then, $xxyzz\in L$ for some $x, z$ satisfying $|x|=|y|=|z|$. Let the state of $\mathbf{M}$ after having read $y$ be $(S, \varphi)$. By construction, $S$ will contain $\sigma'_{x}$ and $\sigma'_{z}$, and $\varphi$ will be $\sigma'_{y}$. Then, 
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > (\sigma'_{z}\circ\sigma'_{z}\circ\varphi \circ\sigma'_{x}\circ\sigma'_{x})(q) & =\sigma'_{xxyzz}(q),
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> > and since $xxyzz\in L$, $\sigma'_{xxyzz}(q)\in F$. Thus, $(S, \varphi)\in \mathbf{F}$.
> > 
> > Conversely, let $\mathbf{M}$ be in an accept state $(S, \varphi)$ after having read $y$. Then, there exist $\alpha, \beta\in S$ and an accept state $q_{f}$ of $M$ such that $(\alpha\alpha\varphi\beta\beta)(q_{0})=q_{f}$. By construction, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are of the form $\sigma'_{z}$ and $\sigma'_{x}$ respectively such that $|x|=|y|=|z|$, and $\varphi=\sigma'_{y}$. Thus, we have $\sigma_{xxyzz}'(q_{0})=q_{f}$, which implies $M$ accepts $xxyzz$.
>

# Problem 3

Let $M=(Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_{0}, F)$ be a DFA recognizing $L$. Define $\mathbf{M}_{\text{max}}=(Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_{0}, \mathbf{F}_{\text{max}})$ by
$$
\mathbf{F}_{\text{max}}:=\{ q:q\in F, \text{ and }\not\exists w\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ such that }\sigma_{w}'(q)\in F \}.
$$
> [!Claim]
> $\mathbf{M}_{\text{max}}$ recognizes $\text{max(L)}$.
> 
> > [!Proof]
> > Let $y\in \text{max(L)}$. Then, there does not exist $w\in L$ such that $w> y$. This is precisely when $\sigma'_{y}(q_{0})\in \mathbf{F}_{\text{max}}$. Similarly, if $\mathbf{M}_{\text{max}}$ accepts $y$, by the construction of $\mathbf{F}_{\text{max}}$ there does not exist $w> y$ such that $M$ accepts $w$.
> 

Define an NFA $\mathbf{M}_{\text{min}}=(Q, \Sigma, \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{min}}, q_{0}, F)$ by
$$
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{min}}(q, a)=\begin{cases}
\{ \delta(q, a) \} & q\not\in F \\
\emptyset & q\in F.
\end{cases}
$$
> [!Claim]
> $\mathbf{M}_{\text{min}}$ recognizes $\text{min}(L)$.
> 
> > [!Proof]
> > Let $y\in \text{min(L)}$. Then, there does not exist $w< y$ such that $M$ accepts $w$, and by the construction of $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{min}}$, $\mathbf{M}_{\text{min}}$ must accept $y$. Conversely, if $\mathbf{M}_{\text{min}}$ accepts $y$, then $\mathbf{M}_{\text{min}}$ cannot have occupied any final state while reading $y$, hence $y\in \text{min(L)}$.
>
